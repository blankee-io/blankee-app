Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Blankee

Getting Started

Blankee is a personal budgeting app that helps you track your income and expenses on a weekly, daily, monthly, or yearly basis. You set up income and expense categories, log entries as you earn and spend, and see your running balance (your "remainder") update in real time. Recurring income and bills are forecast ahead of time, and Blankee asks you to confirm each one as its day arrives.

After registering, you'll be guided through a setup flow where you add your income and expense categories and choose a starting savings balance. Once complete, you'll land on your main dashboard where you can start logging entries right away.

Blankee offers four dashboard views to match how you think about money:

  • Weekly (3-month) — See the last 12 weeks side-by-side. Great for spotting trends.
  • Daily — Drill into a single day to see every entry.
  • Monthly — See each month's totals across several months.
  • Yearly — High-level annual overview.

You can set any view as your home page from your Profile settings.

The remainder is your net balance for a given period — total income minus total expenses. A positive remainder means you came in under budget; a negative number means you spent more than you earned. Each period's remainder also carries forward to the next as a starting point, so your balance stays connected across weeks.

Categories & Entries

Go to Manage Categories from your profile or the dashboard. From there you can add, rename, reorder, or hide income and expense categories. You can also group related categories together for a cleaner dashboard view.

A recurring entry is automatically added to your budget on a schedule you define — weekly, biweekly, monthly, yearly, or on specific days. For example, you might set up a $1,200 rent expense that recurs on the 1st of every month. Blankee will populate that amount on the right date every period, so you don't have to enter it manually.

A bucket is a forecast entry. When you create a recurring entry, Blankee writes one for every future occurrence so your dashboard shows what is coming, not just what has already happened. They count toward your totals and your remainder from the moment they are created.

How a bucket is consumed depends on the type you chose:

  • Wages and Bills are all-or-nothing. One entry settles the whole occurrence, because a bill is either paid or it is not.
  • Variable income and Allowances deplete gradually. Each entry you record reduces what is left for that period, so the bucket shows how much of the allowance remains.

A bucket whose day has arrived is resolved by the confirmation prompt described below, or by recording a matching entry yourself. Nothing removes one silently.

Forecast entries need to become records at some point, and only you know whether they actually happened. At a time you choose, Blankee lists the day's forecast entries and asks about each one:

  • Yes — it happened. The forecast becomes a real entry.
  • Yes, for a different amount — it happened, but not for the figure that was forecast.
  • No — not yet. It moves to tomorrow, and a small badge counts how many days it has been carried.
  • Skip — it is not going to happen, and the entry is removed.

An entry keeps being asked about until you answer it. The only time one disappears without you saying so is when a push lands on a day that already has an entry for that category, in which case the two are merged.

Without a bank feed, nothing corrects the small differences that build up over time — a wage that arrived for a slightly different amount, a cash spend never recorded. On a cadence you set under Settings, Blankee asks what your balances actually are and records the difference.

You are asked separately about your current account, your savings, and each credit card, because they are different kinds of number and nothing is netted across them. Any entries still waiting to be confirmed are confirmed first, and each difference is recorded as a single Uncategorized entry so you can see exactly what was adjusted.

Nothing is changed unless you say yes, and declining costs nothing — the next occurrence asks again. Balances that a connected bank keeps up to date are not offered, since a sync would overwrite whatever you typed.

Bank Connections

Bank connections are temporarily unavailable while we move to a new Open Banking provider. In the meantime you can enter income and expenses manually, and everything you have already recorded stays exactly as it is. We'll re-enable connections from the Bank Accounts page as soon as the new provider is live.

Not in this version. Blankee is built to work with a bank provider, but no provider is enabled by default, so a standard installation records everything by hand. The Bank Accounts menu only appears once an account has been connected.

Everything else works without it: recurring entries forecast what is coming, the confirmation prompt turns those forecasts into records, and the balance reminder keeps Blankee's figures in step with your bank.

Blankee never stores your bank username or password. When a provider is connected, the connection is handled by that provider and Blankee holds only the account details and transactions it returns. Nothing is shared with anyone else.

Credit Accounts

A credit account lets you track spending on a credit card or line of credit separately from your regular expense categories. You add expense categories under the credit account (e.g., Dining, Shopping), log charges against those categories, and track the running balance including interest. Payments to the credit account reduce the balance.

On the weekly dashboard, find the credit account section at the bottom. Click on a cell in the Payment row to enter the amount you paid toward that card. The balance will update automatically to reflect the payment.

A Bud is a dedicated budget project for a specific goal — like tracking a home renovation, a vacation, or any large planned expense. You create a Bud, add line items with amounts and due dates, and Blankee helps you track progress toward the total. Buds connect to expense categories so costs flow into your regular budget too.

Savings & Balance

Blankee calculates your savings by accumulating the remainder from each week. Your starting savings balance is set during profile setup and can be updated in your profile settings. If you connect a bank account, Blankee can also auto-import your actual bank balance as an adjustment to keep your savings figure accurate.

You can set a balance threshold in your Profile settings. When your weekly remainder drops below this number, Blankee will send you a notification so you know to keep an eye on spending. Set it to 0 to disable the alert.

Profile & Settings

In your Profile, scroll to the Settings section and select your preferred view from the Home Page dropdown. Options include the weekly 3-month view, daily view, monthly view, and yearly view. Your selection takes effect immediately.

By default, budget weeks run Saturday → Friday. If you toggle Friday Start Week in your profile settings, weeks will run Friday → Thursday instead. This is useful if your pay cycle or personal planning starts on a Friday. All dashboards and totals will update to reflect the new week boundary.

In your Profile, scroll to the Two-Factor Authentication section. Click Set Up MFA, scan the QR code with an authenticator app (such as Google Authenticator or Authy), then enter the 6-digit code to confirm. Once enabled, you'll be asked for a code each time you log in.

In your Profile, enter your new email address in the email field and save. The change takes effect immediately, and you'll use the new address to sign in from then on.

In your Profile settings, find the Currency dropdown and select your preferred currency. The symbol will update across all dashboards and entry fields.

Notifications

Blankee sends in-app notifications for:

  • Entries to confirm — the day's forecast entries waiting for an answer.
  • Balance reminder — when it is time to reconcile against your real balances.
  • Projected shortfall — when your remainder is set to fall below zero on a future date.
  • Allowance spent — when a recurring allowance is used up, or income arrives above forecast.
  • Account changes — a password reset or a change of email address.

View them all with the bell icon in the top navigation bar. Under Settings › Notifications you can also choose which of these are sent to you by email — they are all on by default, and the Email Notifications switch above them turns email off entirely.
